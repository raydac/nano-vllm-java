# Brothers Grimm

The Brothers Grimm were Jacob Grimm and Wilhelm Grimm.

Jacob Grimm was born on 4 January 1785 in Hanau, Germany.

Wilhelm Grimm was born on 24 February 1786 in Hanau, Germany.

Their father was Philipp Wilhelm Grimm, a lawyer who died in 1796.

After their father's death, an aunt helped them finish their education.

They studied at the Kassel school and then at Marburg University, first intending to become lawyers.

They turned from law toward German language, folklore, and literature.

Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm were very close and preferred to live and work together.

In 1805 Jacob Grimm went to Paris for scholarly work; the brothers disliked being apart.

Jacob Grimm worked for a time as a librarian to Jérôme Bonaparte.

In 1815 Jacob Grimm attended the Congress of Vienna as a representative linked to Kassel.

Jacob Grimm later became a librarian in Kassel; Wilhelm Grimm also worked there as a librarian.

Wilhelm Grimm married in 1825, but the brothers still lived and worked together.

In Göttingen, Jacob Grimm became a professor of German literature and a senior librarian.

Wilhelm Grimm also taught and worked as a librarian in Göttingen and later became a full professor.

The Brothers Grimm were among the Göttingen Seven who protested when King Ernst August of Hanover revoked the
constitution in 1837.

For that protest they were dismissed and had to leave Göttingen within three days.

They later moved to Berlin under the patronage of Frederick William of Prussia.

In Berlin they were elected to the Berlin Academy of Sciences and lectured at Berlin University.

The Brothers Grimm collected and published German fairy tales in Children's and Household Tales (Kinder- und
Hausmärchen).

Their fairy-tale collection includes Little Red Riding Hood (Rotkäppchen), Hansel and Gretel, Snow White, and
Cinderella, among many others.

They also worked on a large German dictionary (Deutsches Wörterbuch) on a scientific basis.

Wilhelm Grimm died on 16 December 1859.

Jacob Grimm died on 20 September 1863.

Jacob Grimm is often described as the more strictly scholarly brother; Wilhelm Grimm shaped many tale texts for young
readers.

Their fairy tales helped make German folklore famous far beyond Germany.
